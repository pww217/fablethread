## Turn 5 — Present the salvaged tech to Joe Salazar for inspection

You reach into your pack and pull out the **Coiled Copper Wire** and the **Heavy-duty Capacitors**, setting them on the damp workbench beside the vibrating filtration unit. The metallic gleam of the pristine components stands in stark contrast to the grime and rust of the Christinafort Water Works. **Joe Salazar** leans over, his calloused hands hovering inches above the tech as if he’s afraid it might vanish if he touches it too quickly. He picks up one of the capacitors, turning it over in his palm, his eyes tracing the unmarred casing with a reverence usually reserved for holy relics.

"Clean," **Joe Salazar** mutters, his voice losing its jagged edge for the first time in days. He sets the component down and looks you dead in the eye, his shoulders finally dropping an inch from their hunched, defensive position. "This isn't just a patch, Michael. This is a lifeline. You might have just bought us another month of breathing easy." He wipes a smear of grease from his forehead, leaving a dark streak across his brow, and exhales a long, shaky breath that seems to carry the weight of the entire settlement's anxiety.

His brief moment of relief is short-lived, however, as his gaze drifts from your find to the sputtering pressure gauges on the main manifold. The relief in his eyes hardens back into a grim, calculating focus. "Don't get too comfortable, though," he says, gesturing toward the dark, damp tunnels leading deeper into the settlement's sub-levels. "The power surge from the repair might have knocked something else loose in the secondary grid. The lights in the infirmary are flickering, and if the cooling pumps for the well-heads fail, all this good work won't mean a damn thing."


## Turn 6 — Use the multimeter to diagnose the power surge impact

You reach for your **Digital Multimeter**, pulling it from your kit and clicking the dial to the appropriate setting. The device feels heavy and reliable in your hand, though you notice the low battery warning flickering on its small, cracked screen. You press the probes against the terminal lugs of the newly installed capacitors, watching the scrolling digits as you test for voltage stability and any residual surges bleeding from the primary line into the secondary grid.

The readings stabilize quickly, showing a clean, consistent flow of power where you need it most. For a brief moment, the constant, frantic hissing of the filtration system settles into a rhythmic, soothing thrum that vibrates through the soles of your boots. The metallic ozone smell in the air seems to soften, replaced by the damp, earthy scent of water moving steadily through fresh pipes. Even **Joe Salazar** seems to pause, his hands resting motionless on the control panel as he listens to the machine find its heartbeat again.

However, the peace is brittle. As you pull the probes away, a sudden, sharp pop echoes from the darkened corridor leading toward the settlement's residential wing. Overhead, the heavy industrial lights in the water works flicker violently, their orange glow stuttering like a dying pulse before dimming to a dull, sickly amber. The vibration in the floorboards shifts from a steady hum to a stuttering, uneven shudder, suggesting the surge you just bypassed has found a new, more vulnerable path to follow.


## Turn 7 — Ask Joe for a beter contract

You lean back against the vibrating workbench, the rhythm of the machine still pulsing through your spine, and catch **Joe Salazar** before he can turn back to the manifold. "The filtration is holding for now, Joe," you say, your voice steady despite the amber gloom of the room. "But if I'm going to be the one diving into the dark to keep these pumps from seizing up again, I need more than just a handful of rations and a 'thank you.' I need a contract that reflects the risk of what's lurking in those sub-levels."

**Joe Salazar** freezes, his hand hovering over a pressure gauge that is beginning to rattle rhythmically against its glass housing. He turns to you, the grim lines of his face etched deep by the dim light. He looks at the **Coiled Copper Wire** and the **Heavy-duty Capacitors** you just installed—proof of your worth—and then back to your eyes. He knows the settlement is on the brink, and he knows he can't afford to lose the only technician who can keep the water flowing.

"You're right," he says, his voice dropping to a low, gravelly rasp. He reaches into his heavy canvas jacket and pulls out a small, weathered pouch, tossing it onto the workbench next to your tools. It lands with a heavy, metallic clink. "I can't give you more rations—the stores are too thin—but I can authorize a Tier-Two scavenging sweep. You'll have access to the central armory for better gear, and if you bring back more high-grade tech like those capacitors, I'll put you on a permanent retainer. You won't be scavenging for scraps anymore; you'll be scavenging for the settlement's future."

As he speaks, a violent, uneven shudder rolls through the floorboards, more pronounced than before. The stuttering rhythm of the filtration system grows more frantic, a mechanical coughing fit that sends a spray of condensation from a nearby pipe. The cooling pumps in the distance are clearly struggling to compensate for the electrical instability, their distant, frantic clatter echoing through the damp walls of the works.


## Turn 8 — Accept the Tier-Two sweep and ask about armory access.

"I'll take the contract," you say, your eyes momentarily drifting to the **Credits** inside the weathered pouch. You reach out, pulling the pouch toward you to secure your new standing. "But if I'm headed into the sub-levels, I need more than just a promise. I need to know how I get into the central armory. Is the access code updated, or do I need a physical key from your office?"

**Joe Salazar** nods, a curt, businesslike motion that shows he respects the negotiation. "The armory's under lock and key, but with this tier, you'll have a clearance token. I'll have **Richard White** bring it to you once we've stabilized this mess. He knows the gear better than anyone." He starts to turn back toward the manifold, but a violent, rhythmic thudding from the pipes above cuts him off.

The mechanical coughing of the filtration system escalates into a series of violent, wet spasms. A nearby junction box groans under the electrical strain, sparks sputtering behind its cracked casing like dying stars. Suddenly, a high-pressure steam line overhead shrieks, the metal screaming under a sudden surge of heat. A hairline fracture splinters across a nearby copper pipe, and a jet of scalding condensation sprays into the air, hissing aggressively against the concrete floor.

The floorboards beneath your boots don't just shudder now; they buck. The rhythmic clatter of the cooling pumps in the distance has transformed into a frantic, dying scream of grinding metal. The amber light overhead strobes violently, casting long, jerking shadows against the walls as the entire room begins to vibrate with a terrifying, uneven frequency. The settlement's lifeline isn't just struggling—it's tearing itself apart.
