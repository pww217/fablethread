## Turn 5 — Present the salvaged tech to Joe Salazar for inspection

You reach into your pack and pull out the **Coiled Copper Wire** and the **Heavy-duty Capacitors**, setting them on the damp workbench beside the vibrating filtration unit. The metallic gleam of the pristine components stands in stark contrast to the grime and rust of the Christinafort Water Works. **Joe Salazar** leans over, his calloused hands hovering inches above the tech as if he’s afraid it might vanish if he touches it too quickly. He picks up one of the capacitors, turning it over in his palm, his eyes tracing the unmarred casing with a reverence usually reserved for holy relics.

"Clean," **Joe Salazar** mutters, his voice losing its jagged edge for the first time in days. He sets the component down and looks you dead in the eye, his shoulders finally dropping an inch from their hunched, defensive position. "This isn't just a patch, Michael. This is a lifeline. You might have just bought us another month of breathing easy." He wipes a smear of grease from his forehead, leaving a dark streak across his brow, and exhales a long, shaky breath that seems to carry the weight of the entire settlement's anxiety.

His brief moment of relief is short-lived, however, as his gaze drifts from your find to the sputtering pressure gauges on the main manifold. The relief in his eyes hardens back into a grim, calculating focus. "Don't get too comfortable, though," he says, gesturing toward the dark, damp tunnels leading deeper into the settlement's sub-levels. "The power surge from the repair might have knocked something else loose in the secondary grid. The lights in the infirmary are flickering, and if the cooling pumps for the well-heads fail, all this good work won't mean a damn thing."


## Turn 6 — Use the multimeter to diagnose the power surge impact

You reach for your **Digital Multimeter**, pulling it from your kit and clicking the dial to the appropriate setting. The device feels heavy and reliable in your hand, though you notice the low battery warning flickering on its small, cracked screen. You press the probes against the terminal lugs of the newly installed capacitors, watching the scrolling digits as you test for voltage stability and any residual surges bleeding from the primary line into the secondary grid.

The readings stabilize quickly, showing a clean, consistent flow of power where you need it most. For a brief moment, the constant, frantic hissing of the filtration system settles into a rhythmic, soothing thrum that vibrates through the soles of your boots. The metallic ozone smell in the air seems to soften, replaced by the damp, earthy scent of water moving steadily through fresh pipes. Even **Joe Salazar** seems to pause, his hands resting motionless on the control panel as he listens to the machine find its heartbeat again.

However, the peace is brittle. As you pull the probes away, a sudden, sharp pop echoes from the darkened corridor leading toward the settlement's residential wing. Overhead, the heavy industrial lights in the water works flicker violently, their orange glow stuttering like a dying pulse before dimming to a dull, sickly amber. The vibration in the floorboards shifts from a steady hum to a stuttering, uneven shudder, suggesting the surge you just bypassed has found a new, more vulnerable path to follow.
