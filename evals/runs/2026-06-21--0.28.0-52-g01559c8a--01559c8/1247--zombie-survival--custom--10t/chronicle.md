
## Turn 0 — Seed

The rhythmic crunch of broken glass under your boots is the only sound in the intersection. A heavy, stagnant heat hangs over the street, carrying the cloying buzz of flies circling a discarded heap near the boarded grocery store. You step around a mattress that has been dragged halfway across the road, its fabric stained and damp from the last rain. To your left, a burned-out car sits like a rusted skeleton, with small, pale saplings pushing through the gaps in its chassis. The swinging door of the grocery store groans on a single hinge, a rhythmic creak that feels like a warning.

You were checking the perimeter near the water crates when you saw him. Raymond Love is standing by the edge of the intersection, his silhouette sharp against the midday sun. He isn't alone. Two residents stand behind him, their eyes fixed on the ground, refusing to meet your gaze. Raymond looks older than he did a month ago; the lines around his mouth have deepened into permanent trenches. He watches you with a clinical detachment that feels more like a judgment than a greeting.

"You shouldn't be out here without a permit, Edwin," Raymond says, his voice devoid of the warmth it once held when you two stood shoulder-to-shoulder in the breach. "The council has issued new directives regarding unauthorized patrols. We cannot have former officers wandering the streets and causing confusion."

Behind him, a small crowd begins to gather near the corner. They aren't looking at the water crates or the broken storefronts; they are looking at Jimmy Snyder, who is standing atop a rusted crate. Jimmy is gesturing wildly toward the sky, his voice carrying a frantic, melodic quality that cuts through the heavy air. The residents aren't listening to Raymond's warnings; they are leaning in toward Jimmy, their faces illuminated by a terrifying sort of hope. You realize then that the authority you once commanded hasn't just slipped away—it has been replaced. The tension in the street is no longer about the scarcity of water or the broken roads; it is the electric, vibrating energy of a community deciding to follow a different master. Raymond steps closer to you, his eyes narrowing. "The people need stability, Edwin. Not ghosts from a dead world. If you continue this, we will have no choice but to treat you as a trespasser."


## Turn 1 — I slip into the shadows near the Council Chambers to eavesdrop on the evening session.

You turn away from Raymond Love and the growing crowd, slipping into the narrow gap between a collapsed storefront and a rusted corrugated fence. You move with practiced silence, weaving through the skeletal remains of street furniture to avoid the eyes of the two residents flanking the Elder. The heavy heat presses against your skin as you navigate the shadows, keeping low and avoiding the open expanse of the intersection where Jimmy Snyder’s frantic preaching continues to draw a crowd.

You reach the Council Chambers, a reinforced concrete structure that once served as a local administrative office. The heavy steel-reinforced doors are shut tight, but the sound of muffled, rhythmic shouting leaks through the seams. You press your back against the cool, grit-covered wall and lean toward a small, cracked window near the base of the entrance.

Inside, the room is dimly lit by a single flickering lantern. Four council members sit around a heavy oak table that has been scarred by years of neglect. Raymond Love sits at the head, his jaw set in a hard line as he leans over a stack of handwritten ledgers. Opposite him, a woman with graying hair and sharp, piercing eyes—Councilwoman Elena Vance—slams her palm against the wood.

"The water levels in the southern reservoir are dropping faster than the gauges suggest," Elena Vance says, her voice a harsh rasp that cuts through the room. "If we don't tighten the rations by tonight, there won't be enough for the guards by week's end."

Raymond Love does not look up from the ledgers. "We cannot tighten them further without sparking a riot, Elena. Not with Snyder feeding them visions of a coming deluge. If we squeeze them now, they will turn to him entirely."


## Turn 2 — I press my ear closer to the window and attempt to catch more details from the ledgers Raymond is studying.

You press your ear against the grit-covered concrete, straining to hear the rustle of paper and the low murmurs from within the chamber. The ledgers remain a mystery; the heavy thud of Elena Vance’s palm and the muffled shouting outside drown out the specific numbers Raymond Love is reviewing.

"Then we lock the southern reservoir gates immediately," Elena Vance declares, her voice cutting through the room with a sharp, decisive edge. She leans forward, her piercing eyes fixed on the ledgers. "If the gauges are lying to us, we stop the outflow and secure the perimeter. We cannot risk a breach while the crowds are this restless."

Raymond Love finally looks up, his jaw tightening as he stares at the grain of the oak table. "Locking the gates now is a provocation, Elena. It tells them we're hiding something."

"It tells them we are surviving," she snaps back.

You reach for the heavy iron latch on the chamber door, your fingers curling around the cold metal with practiced stillness. The mechanism is rusted and grit-caked, but you manipulate it with a slow, deliberate tension that prevents the clatter of shifting iron. Inside the chamber, the four council members remain absorbed in their dispute; Elena Vance is still leaning over the oak table, her hands braced against the wood as she stares down Raymond Love. The heavy thud of her palm from the previous moment still seems to echo in the small space between you and the door.


## Turn 4 — I slowly ease the chamber door open just enough to peer through the crack.

You ease the heavy door open a mere inch, creating a sliver of darkness that allows you to peer into the chamber. Four council members remain seated around the scarred oak table, their silhouettes cast long and jagged by the flickering overhead light. Elena Vance stands rigid at the head of the table, her piercing eyes fixed on a small, leather-bound ledger resting between her and Raymond Love.

Elena slams a finger down onto a specific page in the ledger, the sound sharp as a pistol shot in the tense room. 

"The southern reservoir is nearly dry, Raymond," Elena Vance says, her voice dropping to a low, dangerous hiss that cuts through the previous shouting. "The numbers we gave the districts are a fantasy. If we don't lock down the southern sector immediately, there won't be enough for the central wells by week's end."

Raymond Love leans forward, his weathered face tightening as he stares at the ink-stained pages. The other three council members exchange frantic, wide-eyed glances, their authority seemingly evaporating in the face of her declaration.


## Turn 5 — I pull back from the door and slip into the shadows of the corridor, searching for a secondary entrance or a ventilation duct to gain a better vantage point.

You pull back from the sliver of light and retreat into the heavy shadows of the corridor, your boots making no sound on the grit-covered floor. You press your back against the damp wall and begin a frantic search for another way in, fingers trailing along the peeling wallpaper and searching for the edge of a ventilation duct or a secondary service door.

Behind the heavy wood of the chamber door, the tension snaps. A sharp, jagged sob breaks the silence—one of the four council members has begun weeping openly, their grief a raw, uncontrolled sound that cuts through Elena Vance's clinical authority. The crying is rhythmic and desperate, signaling a total collapse of composure among the officials.

"Stop that at once!" Elena Vance snaps, her voice trembling despite her attempt at steel. 

The weeping only intensifies, joined by the frantic shuffling of chairs against the floorboards as the remaining council members react to the emotional outburst. The room is no longer a place of governance; it has become a chamber of panic.


## Turn 6 — I press my ear against the door, listening intently to identify exactly which council member is breaking down and what Vance says in response.

You press your ear against the heavy wood of the chamber door, straining to pierce through the chaotic din. The weeping is coming from a man—one of the four council members—whose breath comes in jagged, hitching gasps that suggest a total loss of control.

"Enough!" Elena Vance shouts, her voice dropping into a low, dangerous hiss that cuts through the crying. "If you cannot maintain your dignity while we discuss the survival of this entire district, then leave us. Your hysterics are a luxury we cannot afford."

The sound of a heavy chair scraping harshly against the floorboards follows her command, followed by the heavy thud of a body retreating toward the exit. The door handle rattles violently, and for a second, you freeze against the wall. The latch clicks open, and the door swings inward with a sudden, jarring force.

The council member—a man with thinning hair and trembling hands—stumbles into the corridor. He doesn't see you in the shadows; he only sees the exit. He pushes past you blindly, his eyes bloodshot and wide with terror, and bolts down the hallway toward the main intersection. His frantic footsteps echo loudly against the damp walls, drawing attention to your position.


## Turn 7 — I step out from the shadows and give chase after the council member.

You step out from the shadows and sprint after the man with thinning hair. His frantic, uneven footfalls slap against the damp floorboards as he disappears around a corner, leaving you to navigate the narrow corridor alone. You push through the heavy chamber doors just as they begin to swing shut, your boots thudding hard against the cracked asphalt of the Saundersburgh Intersection.

The man is already a distance ahead, weaving erratically between rusted vehicle husks and encroaching vines. He ignores the stares of two nearby residents—a woman with a heavy burlap sack and a man leaning on a makeshift cane—who pause their work to watch his panicked flight. His weeping hasn't stopped; it has transformed into a series of breathless, terrified sobs that carry through the stagnant air.

He reaches the edge of the intersection and veers sharply toward a cluster of overgrown buildings near the perimeter. The chase pulls you away from the council chamber and deep into the winding, debris-choked streets where the shadows of the old world loom large.

You draw your Mended Sidearm, the heavy duct tape on the grip feeling tacky against your palm as you break into a sprint. The man trips over a discarded piece of corrugated metal, the clatter loud and jarring in the quiet street, but he scrambles back up without looking behind him. He ducks behind a collapsed storefront, disappearing into the dark maw of a gutted apothecary.

The four residents—the tall man with a scarred brow, the short man with nervous hands, the woman in a torn shawl, and the youth holding a wooden staff—all turn their heads toward you simultaneously. The residents begin a soft, synchronized chant that vibrates through the narrow alleyway. The atmosphere shifts from stunned silence to a heavy, expectant fervor that makes the air feel thick and claustrophobic.

The chanting grows louder, drowning out the council member's cries and creating a wall of sound that pushes you back toward the street. Driven by the sudden intensity of the crowd and the need to avoid becoming a target for their growing zealotry, you back away from the makeshift altar and retreat from the alleyway. You push through the tangle of vines and debris, exiting the shrine's immediate circle and returning to the open expanse of the Saundersburgh Intersection.
