
## Turn 0 — Seed

The rhythmic clatter of a typewriter with a stuck 'e' key punctuates the heavy silence of the bullpen. You sit at your desk, the floor beneath your feet still slick from the rain that lashed against the windows an hour ago. Smoke hangs in thick, gray ribbons under the flickering ceiling lights, catching the pale glow of the streetlamps outside. The smell of stale coffee and cheap tobacco clings to your coat like a second skin.

You are staring at a ledger when the heavy oak door slams open. Donald Miller stumbles in, his face ashen, clutching a crumpled telegram. He doesn't head for the Sergeant's office; he heads straight for you, ignoring the startled glances of the other officers. Behind him, Roger Sterling follows, looking less like a reporter and more like a man who has seen a ghost. Sterling’s usual cocky stride is gone, replaced by a frantic, twitching energy.

'Jerome, you need to see this,' Miller says, his voice cracking as he drops the telegram onto your desk, right over your neatly organized notes. The paper is damp. 'They took him. Right from the newsroom.'

Sterling leans over your desk, his shadow falling across your ledger. He doesn't wait for an invitation. 'The editor of the Gazette,' Sterling whispers, his eyes darting toward the door to ensure no other officers are listening too closely. 'They didn't just grab him, Vance. They left a note. A demand for the city council to lift the printing embargo or they release the ledger from the dockyard scandal.'

You look down at the telegram. The ink is smudged, but the words are clear: *The truth has been silenced; now we silence the voice of the truth.*

Miller leans closer, his voice dropping to a low growl. 'If this gets out to the public before we find him, the Commissioner will have my badge for breakfast, and you'll be looking for work in a gutter. The council is already breathing down our necks about the dockyard strikes. We can't afford a kidnapping scandal on top of everything else.'

Sterling taps his finger nervously against the wood of your desk. 'I can help you find him, Vance. I have contacts near the waterfront that the precinct doesn't know about. But you owe me for that business down at the pier last winter. It's time to settle up.'

You realize the gravity of the situation immediately. This isn't just a kidnapping; it is a direct strike against the city's fragile information control. If the editor dies or the ledger is leaked, the entire power structure of East Jonathanton will crumble, and you will be caught in the middle of the wreckage.


## Turn 1 — Examine the faded photograph for any background clues or landmarks.

You reach into your coat pocket and pull out the *Faded Photograph*, setting it on the desk beside the damp telegram. You hold the small, worn image up to the flickering light of the bullpen ceiling to scan the background for any detail that might tie back to the kidnapping or the dockyard scandal mentioned by Sterling.

The photo shows a young man in a crisp dockyard uniform, standing stiffly against a backdrop of heavy industrial cranes and massive wooden crates. Behind him, the silhouette of a specific pier structure rises against a gray sky—a distinctive, three-tiered warehouse with a slanted roof that you recognize from your previous investigations near the waterfront. A sign hangs limply from one of the warehouse loading bays, though the text is too weathered to read clearly in the dim light.


## Turn 2 — I grab my coat and head toward the waterfront.

Jerome Vance grabs his coat from the back of the chair and strides out of the bullpen, leaving the *Faded Photograph* on the desk beneath the flickering light. Donald Miller looks up from a stack of intake forms, his scowl deepening as he watches you depart without a word of explanation. Roger Sterling leans forward from his seat, ink staining his knuckles as he calls after you, "Vance! You can't just walk out while the city is burning!"

The heavy precinct doors swing shut behind you, cutting off the stale smell of coffee and tobacco for the damp, salt-heavy air of the street. You navigate the narrow corridors toward the waterfront, passing two patrol officers who stand guard near a barricade to manage the strike-related congestion. The transition from the cramped station to the open docks brings a sudden chill that bites through your coat.

The pier you recognized from the photo looms ahead through the gray mist of the harbor. As you approach the three-tiered warehouse with the slanted roof, you notice a heavy wooden crate sitting near the loading bay where the sign once hung. A single, fresh smear of crimson paint—or perhaps blood—stains the corner of the crate, and tucked beneath its splintered edge is a discarded scrap of newsprint from *The Gazette*.


## Turn 3 — Pick up the scrap of newsprint and examine it closely.

Jerome Vance stoops to retrieve the scrap of newsprint from beneath the splintered edge of the heavy wooden crate. The dampness of the pier has made the paper limp, causing the ink to bleed slightly into the gray fibers. Holding the fragment up against the dim light filtering through the mist, you find more than just a discarded remnant of *The Gazette*.

> ...not until the warehouse door is breached... the price for silence is the embargo...

The text is fragmentary, but the handwriting is hurried and heavy-handed. Beneath the printed masthead, several lines of handwritten notes scrawl across the margin in dark, smudged ink. The words suggest a direct link between the kidnapping of the editor and the ongoing strike tensions at these very docks.


## Turn 4 — Examine the handwritten notes more closely to look for any specific warehouse numbers or landmarks.

Jerome Vance holds the damp *Gazette* scrap closer to his eyes, squinting through the gray mist to decipher the hurried scrawl beneath the printed ink. You trace the heavy-handed strokes of the pen, looking for any sign of a location or a specific identifier that could tie these notes to a physical spot on the pier.

The smudged ink reveals more than just the ransom demand; tucked into the very corner of the margin, beneath the mention of the embargo, are three digits scratched in a frantic hand:

> 4-B

Beside the numbers, a small, crude sketch of an anchor entwined with a broken gear sits in the margin—a specific mark used by the heavy stevedores to label certain storage units. The combination points directly to Warehouse 4-B, located at the far end of this pier where the shadow of the massive grain elevator looms over the tracks.


## Turn 5 — I check the cylinder of my service revolver and head toward Warehouse 4-B.

Jerome Vance swings open the cylinder of his Service Revolver to ensure all six rounds are seated firmly before snapping the steel shut with a heavy click. He turns away from the blood-stained crate and begins walking toward the far end of the pier, passing the Two patrol officers who stand rigid behind their barricade. The officers do not look up from their task of directing the flow of striking workers, their eyes fixed on the congestion in the narrow corridors.

The mist thickens as Jerome approaches the shadow cast by the massive grain elevator. Warehouse 4-B sits at the terminus of the tracks, a hulking structure of weathered timber and corrugated iron that seems to absorb the gray light of the morning. As he nears the heavy sliding doors of the warehouse, he notices a series of fresh disturbances in the damp sawdust covering the ground.

Beside the entrance to 4-B, a heavy wooden crate lies splintered on its side, and several dozen discarded matchsticks lie scattered around a dark, viscous smear on the doorframe. A single, heavy boot print—deeper than a standard stride—is pressed into the mud leading toward a side service door that hangs slightly ajar.
