You can feel the biting chill as the bay depressurizes for the docking cycle, making your breath bloom in thin, white clouds under the flickering emergency lighting. Ricardo Smith steps into the light, his Coalition uniform crisp despite the grime of New Rickyfort. He doesn't look at your face; he looks at the manifest, then at the heavy crates stowed behind the damaged pallet. Behind him, two armed guards stand motionless, their hands resting near their sidearms as they scan the shadows of the bay. The air feels heavy, not just from the pressure change, but from the weight of a history you both thought was buried under layers of official paperwork.

'You are running late, Andrew,' Ricardo says, his voice devoid of warmth. He taps a stylus against his datapad, the sound echoing sharply in the hollow space. 'And your manifest is missing the secondary stabilization seals for these catalysts. That is a Class Four violation.'

He looks up then, his eyes meeting yours with a look that isn't quite accusation, but it certainly isn't friendship. He knows exactly what is hidden beneath those crates—the same kind of cargo that kept both of you fed when the rim was nothing but dust and desperation. One of the guards shifts their weight, the metal of their boots clanking against the deck plating, a sound that feels much too loud in the silence. The tension is a physical thing, pulling tight between you like a fraying cable. Ricardo leans in closer, lowering his voice so the guards can't hear, but his eyes remain as cold as the depressurizing air. 'I can look the other way for a fee, or I can call for a full sweep. You know how the Coalition is feeling about these terraforming shipments lately. They are looking for any excuse to tighten the noose.'

You realize then that the lockdown isn't just about resources; it is about control. Every breath taken in this sector is being monitored, every shipment scrutinized. If they find what you are carrying, it won't just be your ship they seize—it will be your life.
