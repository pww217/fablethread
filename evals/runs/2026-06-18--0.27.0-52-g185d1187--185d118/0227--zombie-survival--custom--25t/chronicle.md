
## Turn 0 — Seed

The rhythmic rustle of dry corn stalks in the wind is the only sound breaking the heavy heat of the afternoon. You lean against a tractor with its battery pulled long ago, the metal hot and pitted beneath your palms. The air smells of dust and something sharper—the metallic tang of approaching rain that will never actually arrive to wash away the grime.

To your left, the fence line is down in three places, letting the weeds and thistle creep further into what used to be a managed field. You scan the perimeter, eyes searching for any sign of movement that isn't the wind-blown stalks. This sector was supposed to be empty, a dead zone between the settlement and the military checkpoints, but the silence feels wrong today. It is too heavy, weighted by the knowledge that every scrap you find here is being watched.

Scott Baxter emerges from behind a cluster of overgrown bushes, his eyes darting toward the broken fence line before settling on you. He isn't carrying much, just a tattered satchel and a look of intense agitation that makes your chest tighten. He doesn't wait for you to speak; he simply points toward the root cellar door down the slope, which has been bolted from the outside with heavy iron bars.

"They were here, Morgan," Scott says, his voice low and jagged. "I saw the tracks near the wellhead. Not scavenger tracks—these were heavy, rhythmic. Militia boots."

He moves closer, his hand resting nervously on the hilt of a rusted knife at his belt. He looks less like a partner and more like a man who is ready to bolt if things turn south. "They didn't just pass through. They took the crates we marked for the enclave. The ones with the filtration mesh."

A low, rhythmic thumping begins to vibrate through the ground—the distant, unmistakable drone of an automated patrol unit approaching from the north. It isn't a passing shadow; it is a directed movement. Scott grips your arm, his knuckles white. "If they find us here scavenging without a pass, they won't just take the supplies. They'll take us to the processing center in Port Lori."

You look from Scott to the bolted cellar door. The tension between the need for those filters and the threat of the approaching patrol pulls at you like a physical weight. If you head for the cellar, you might find what was stolen, but you'll be caught in the open when the drones arrive. If you retreat now, the enclave goes without clean water by morning.

Scott's eyes are wide, searching yours for a decision. He is waiting for your signal, the one you've used a hundred times before, but for the first time, the signal feels like it might be a death sentence.
