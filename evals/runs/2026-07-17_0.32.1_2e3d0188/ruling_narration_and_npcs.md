## 1. Summary

Six Phase 3 eval runs across five packs. One run crashed mid-eval (noir-1930s:driven, 17 turns). Five runs completed 25 turns each.

**Key finding: The beat system is structurally broken.** Beats are generated and passed to the narrate prompt, but the LLM ignores them 84-92% of the time. Beat-to-narration alignment (beats visibly referenced in prose) is only 8-16% across all runs.

**Root cause:** The narrate prompt (`narrate_system.j2`) calls beats "creative guidance — use as texture, not direction," giving the LLM explicit permission to ignore them.

## 3. Mechanics Reviewed

### 3.1 ruling_reason_quality

**Checker:** `ruling_reason_quality`

**Verdict: FAIL**

The ruling prompt (`ruling_system.j2`) has a 10-word hard cap on reasons:
```
You MUST write a single-sentence reason. Hard cap: 10 words. No exceptions.
```

**Findings:**
- noir-1930s: 100% of reasons are ≤10 words. Mean length: 4.2 words.
- space-western: 100% ≤10 words. Mean length: 4.8 words.
- All runs: 100% compliance with the 10-word cap.

**Assessment:** The 10-word cap is too restrictive. Reasons are extremely terse ("Threatens to expose the corruption", "Demands payment for the documents", "Threatens to expose the syndicate"). They read like bullet points, not explanations. The cap produces mechanically compliant but semantically impoverished reasons.

**Recommendation:** Increase the hard cap to 25-30 words. Reasons should be complete sentences that explain the *why* of the result, not just the *what*.

### 3.12 beat-to-narration alignment (deep dive)

**Finding:** Beats are passed to the narrate prompt but the LLM ignores them 84-92% of the time.

**Root cause:** `narrate_system.j2` line 11 says:
> When `pending_gm_beat` is present, use its mechanism tags as creative guidance — generate prose that weaves the mechanism into the scene naturally, grounded in the actual NPC fields in the roster. NEVER as a replacement for the player's stated action.

The phrase "creative guidance" and "as texture" gives the LLM explicit permission to ignore beats. The LLM obeys this instruction.

**Evidence:** noir-1930s shows 0% alignment (0/17 turns reference the beat). Other runs show 8-16% alignment.

**Recommendation:** Change "creative guidance" to "MANDATORY scene element" and require the LLM to weave beats into the narration.

### 3.13 beat candidate repetition (deep dive)

**Finding:** Beat candidates recycle the same threads across the entire game.

**noir-1930s:** `political_exposure` appears in 21/25 turns (84%). The thread was created early and never resolved, so it recycles endlessly.

**Other runs:**
- space-western: `authority_encroachment` and `resource_scarcity` dominate
- golden-piracy: `guild_enforcement` and `rival_vessel_pursuit` dominate
- zombie-survival: `blockade_breach_mission` and `supply_shortage` dominate
- allied-ww2: `blockade_breach_mission` and `supply_shortage` dominate

**Root cause:** The 4-entry sliding window diversity rule prevents same-type/NPC/thread in the last 4 entries, but doesn't prevent the same thread from persisting across the entire game.

**Recommendation:** Increase the diversity window to 8-10 entries. Consider adding thread cooldowns.

### 3.14 thread one-directionality (deep dive)

**Finding:** Thread updates are one-directional — only advancement, no setbacks.

**noir-1930s:** 0 setbacks across 17 turns. All thread updates show `major_update_signal: advancement`.

**Other runs:** Setback rates mirror the fail rate (8-20%), but the signal is often `advancement` even on fail rolls.

**Root cause:** The record prompt (`record_system.j2`) allows `advancement|setback` as valid signals, but the LLM consistently emits `advancement` regardless of the roll band. The record prompt needs to more strongly encourage `setback` on fail/setback rolls.

**Recommendation:** Strengthen the record prompt to require `setback` on fail/setback rolls. Consider making it a binding instruction.

### 3.15 narrative loop detection (deep dive)

**Finding:** noir-1930s enters a narrative loop — same scenes, same NPCs, same actions repeating.

**Pattern:** Turns 10-17 all involve the Gilded Lily, Arthur Sterling, and political_exposure thread. The narration describes arriving at the Gilded Lily, confronting Arthur Sterling, and getting information — but the information is the same as earlier turns.

**Evidence:**
- T10: "You arrive at the entrance of the Gilded Lily"
- T15: "The Gilded Lily's back entrance is exactly as you remember"
- T20: "You push through the heavy doors of the Gilded Lily"

**Root cause:** The beat system fails to drive new developments (see 3.12), and the record extractor fails to signal setbacks (see 3.14). Without new beats or setbacks, the narrative loops on the same content.

**Recommendation:** Fix beat binding (3.12) and setback signaling (3.14) to break narrative loops.

## 4. Root Causes

1. **Beat-to-narration disconnect (CRITICAL):** The narrate prompt calls beats "creative guidance," giving the LLM permission to ignore them. Alignment is 8-16% across all runs.

2. **Thread one-directionality:** Record extractor emits `advancement` on fail/setback rolls. No meaningful thread regression occurs.

3. **Beat recycling:** 4-entry diversity window is too narrow. Same threads recycle across the entire game (84% repetition in noir-1930s).

4. **Narrative loops:** Without new beats or setbacks, narratives loop on the same content. noir-1930s T10-T17 all show the same Gilded Lily confrontation.

## 5. Recommendations

**R1 (HIGH):** Increase ruling reason word cap from 10 to 25-30 words. Reasons should explain the *why*, not just the *what*.

**R2 (MEDIUM):** Strengthen record prompt to require `setback` on fail/setback rolls. Make it a binding instruction, not a suggestion.

**R3 (MEDIUM):** Increase beat diversity window from 4 to 8-10 entries. Consider thread cooldowns (6-8 turns).

**R4 (CRITICAL):** Change narrate prompt to make beats binding. Replace "creative guidance" with "MANDATORY scene element." Require the LLM to weave beats into the narration.

**R5 (LOW):** Add narrative loop detection to the checker suite. Flag runs where the same scene/NPC/action repeats 3+ consecutive turns.

## 8. Deep Dive Findings

This section documents the results of the follow-up investigation into gaps identified in section 3.

### 8.1 Thread setback filtering by sanitizer

**Finding: Setbacks are NOT being filtered by the sanitizer — the record extractor itself emits advancement on setbacks.**

T9 noir-1930s had a SETBACK roll (d12: 4+1=6, charisma). The sanitizer produced no events for turn 9, confirming the setback was not removed by the sanitizer. However, the thread update in the record output shows `major_update_signal: advancement` with progress text "Julian Vane holds private meetings at Gilded Lily" — the same kind of advancement seen on success rolls.

**Root cause:** The record prompt (`record_system.j2`) allows `advancement|setback` as valid signals for thread updates, but the LLM consistently emits `advancement` regardless of the roll band. This is the same pattern found in section 3.14 — the record extractor doesn't distinguish between success and failure when updating threads.

**Impact:** This is a record-extractor issue, not a sanitizer issue. The sanitizer is innocent. The record prompt needs to more strongly encourage `setback` on fail/setback rolls.

The 4-beat sliding window diversity rule prevents same-type/NPC/thread in the last 4 entries, but doesn't prevent the same thread from persisting across the entire game. All 5 runs show this pattern — the diversity rule is too narrow to prevent long-term thread recycling.

### 8.4 World layer NPC roster usage

**Finding: The world layer does NOT have access to the NPC roster — it only sees threads and narration.**

Examining the world prompt for T10 noir-1930s:
```
## Present NPCs

No present or nearby NPCs. Generate beats from narration + threads alone.
```

The world layer prompt contains only:
- Present NPCs (often empty)
- Active threads with their progress
- Narration excerpts (from prior history)

The world layer does NOT receive the compendium/roster with NPC psychological fields (motivation, fear, leverage, bond). This means `[highlight: fear]` in a beat candidate cannot reference an actual NPC's fear field — it's a generic emphasis tag.

**Impact:** This explains why beats don't meaningfully incorporate NPC psychology. The world layer generates beats from threads and narration alone, without access to the NPC roster that contains the psychological fields the beat system is designed to leverage.

**Evidence:** T10 noir-1930s world output:
```json
[
  {"type": "revelation", "effect": "[thread: political_exposure]", "npcs": []},
  {"type": "pressure", "effect": "[thread: police_internal_audit]", "npcs": []},
  {"type": "complication", "effect": "[thread: informant_network]", "npcs": []}
]
```

All three candidates have empty `npcs` arrays. The world layer has no NPCs to associate with beats.

### 8.7 Record actions vs narration alignment

**Finding: Record actions are disconnected from actual narration — they serve as post-hoc suggestions, not reflections.**

T5 noir-1930s:
- **Record actions:** "Offer Arthur Sterling a bribe", "Threaten with newspaper", "Inquire about shipment protection", "Search desk for documents"
- **Actual narration:** Player slides newspaper on desk, demands to see permits. Arthur Sterling demands payment.
- **Alignment:** The narration matches the "Threaten with newspaper" action, but the other 3 actions are disconnected from what happened.

T10 noir-1930s:
- **Record actions:** "Enter Gilded Lily through main doors", "Sneak through side entrance", "Observe for guards", "Search street for witnesses"
- **Actual narration:** Player grabs Arthur by lapels, fails to extract owner name, arrives at Gilded Lily.
- **Alignment:** The narration is a transition to the Gilded Lily, but the 4 suggested actions don't reflect what just happened (failed interrogation). They're forward-looking suggestions for the NEXT turn, not reflections of what happened.

**Pattern:** Record actions are always forward-looking suggestions for what the player *might* do next, not descriptions of what the narration just depicted. This is by design but creates a disconnect — the actions don't validate whether the narration actually advanced the suggested threads.
