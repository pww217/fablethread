
## Turn 0 — Seed

The heavy, metallic scent of wet wool and stale coffee hangs thick in the air, clinging to the back of your throat. You sit at a scarred wooden desk, the rhythmic, aggressive clatter of the teletype machine providing a relentless heartbeat to the silence of the precinct records room. Smoke from a dozen forgotten cigarettes curls lazily under the flickering ceiling lights, creating hazy islands in the dimness. The floor beneath your boots is slick, stained by the rain that has been tracking in from the streets all evening, leaving dark, muddy patches near the heavy iron door.

You stare down at the file spread before you, but your eyes keep drifting to the padlocked bottom drawer of the filing cabinet across the room. It stands there like a silent sentry, guarding secrets that don't belong in a public ledger. You know what you saw earlier—the way the ink on the witness statement looked too fresh, too deliberate, as if someone had sat here just an hour ago with a fountain pen and a heavy conscience. The official report says the suspect was alone, but the photograph tucked into your pocket tells a different story, one involving two men and a struggle that never made it into the paper.

The door creaks open, and the heavy tread of boots echoes against the linoleum. Matthew Ross walks in, his shoulders slumped under the weight of his overcoat. He doesn't look at you at first; he just heads for the coffee urn, his movements slow and heavy, like a man walking through deep water. He looks older than he did when you were both digging foxholes in France, the lines around his eyes etched deep by years of seeing things that cannot be unseen.

'You should be home, Robert,' Matthew says, his voice a low rasp that barely carries over the teletype's chatter. He pours a cup of coffee, the liquid dark and oily, and finally turns to face you. His eyes linger on the document you are holding, and for a second, the weary mask slips, revealing a flash of something sharp and panicked. He steps closer, leaning his hands on your desk, his shadow stretching long and jagged across your paperwork. 'Some things are better left unread. Some mistakes are easier to live with if they stay buried in the archives.'

He isn't talking about the weather. He is looking directly at the discrepancy you just uncovered, and the silence that follows is heavier than the rain outside. You can see the tension in his jaw, the way his fingers twitch near his holster. He is a man caught between the loyalty he owes a brother-in-arms and the duty he owes a system that is clearly rotting from the inside out. Outside, a heavy rain begins to lash against the windowpanes, punctuating the tension of the room. You realize then that Matthew isn't just here to tell you to go home; he is here to see if you have already seen too much.


## Turn 1 — I examine the crime scene photograph for any inconsistencies that might suggest tampered evidence.

You pull the *Faded Crime Scene Photograph* from your pocket and spread it flat against the scarred wood of the desk, squinting under the flickering light to find a flaw in the image. You trace the lines of the struggle, searching for a smudge of fresh ink or a shadow that doesn't align with the official narrative, but the grainy texture of the print obscures any fine detail. The heavy paper feels cold and damp against your fingertips, offering no clarity, only the frustrating blur of a poorly developed shot.

Matthew Ross stands over you, his shadow swallowing the photograph and plunging your work into even deeper gloom. He doesn't move to stop you, but his presence is a physical weight, his eyes fixed on your hands rather than the image itself. 

The teletype machine suddenly shrieks, its mechanical teeth biting into a fresh roll of paper with a violent, rhythmic stutter that cuts through the tension. From the corner of the room, Joseph Vance looks up from his stacks of parchment, his eyes darting toward Matthew and then quickly down to his ledger, his hands trembling as he adjusts a stack of folders. 

"The rain is getting worse," Matthew says, his voice dropping an octave, sounding less like a warning and more like a plea. He reaches out, his hand hovering just inches from yours on the desk, though he does not touch you. "Go home, Robert. Don't let a scrap of paper ruin a good man's life."


## Turn 2 — I ignore Matthew and walk over to Joseph Vance's desk to inspect the folders he is so nervously adjusting.

Robert Sullivan stands, leaving the photograph and Matthew Ross behind, and crosses the cramped room toward Joseph Vance's desk. As Robert approaches, Joseph Vance flinches, his twitching hands catching the edge of a heavy stack of manila folders; they slide off the desk and hit the floor with a thunderous, paper-heavy crash that rings through the records room. 

Joseph Vance freezes, eyes wide and darting toward Matthew Ross, his breath coming in shallow, jagged hitches. He doesn't reach for the fallen files immediately, instead staring at the mess on the floor as if it were a crime scene itself. 

Among the scattered documents, one specific folder lies face-up, its heavy cardstock partially slid out of its sleeve. Robert catches a glimpse of a stamped header that stands out from the rest: 

> CASE FILE #402 - HOMICIDE - OFFICIAL RECORD

The folder is noticeably cleaner, the edges crisp and unstained, contrasting sharply with the dusty, yellowed files surrounding it. Matthew Ross turns his head toward the sound of the crash, his heavy eyes shifting from Robert to the fallen stack, his jaw tightening as he maintains his position by the desk.


## Turn 3 — I pick up Case File #402 and inspect it for signs of tampering or recent handling.

Robert Sullivan reaches down and retrieves CASE FILE #402 from the floor, lifting the heavy cardstock sleeve to examine the contents. He flips through the pages, checking for fresh ink, paper fibers that don't match the age of the document, or any signs that specific sheets were swapped into this suspiciously clean folder. 

The documents inside are immaculate, lacking even a single smudge of graphite or the inevitable coffee rings that mark most files in this room. As Robert inspects the margins and the binding, he notes that the paper quality is slightly superior to the surrounding archives, suggesting it was processed far more recently than its filing date implies. 

Joseph Vance remains frozen by his desk, his eyes fixed on the folder in Robert's hands, while Matthew Ross steps closer, his heavy boots thudding against the floorboards as he looms over the scene.


## Turn 4 — I pull out my leather-bound notebook and begin cross-referencing the page numbers and dates in Case File #402 against the visible documents on the surrounding desks to see which specific reports are missing or replaced.

Robert Sullivan pulls the *Leather Bound Notebook* from his pocket and flips to a fresh page, scribbling down the dates and sequence numbers visible within *Case File #402*. He begins a methodical comparison, eyes darting between the immaculate pages in his hands and the surrounding stacks of dusty, coffee-stained reports on the nearby desks. 

The discrepancy is immediate and glaring. While the folder claims to document a homicide from three years ago, the specific supplemental reports for ballistics and autopsy—documents that should be nested within this sequence—are absent from the visible archives, replaced by newer, thinner sheets that don't match the chronological progression of the surrounding files. 

A heavy, calloused hand drops onto Robert’s shoulder, the weight of it grounding him mid-notation. Matthew Ross leans in closer, his shadow stretching over the notebook and the suspicious folder alike. The scent of stale tobacco follows him as he lowers his voice to a low, warning growl that vibrates through Robert's collarbone.

"Drop it, Robert," Matthew Ross says, his eyes darting toward Joseph Vance, who is still staring wide-eyed from behind the desk. "Some things are better left buried under the dust where they belong."


## Turn 5 — I slowly close the notebook and slide Case File #402 back into its place, keeping my eyes fixed on Matthew Ross.

Robert Sullivan slowly closes the *Leather Bound Notebook* and slides *Case File #402* back into its designated slot among the heavy iron cabinets, keeping his eyes fixed on Matthew Ross. He does not look away from the detective's weary, heavy-lidded gaze, even as the paper scrapes against the metal shelf with a dry, final sound.

Matthew Ross maintains his grip on Robert’s shoulder for several seconds after the folder is returned, his thumb pressing into the fabric of Robert's coat. He exhales a plume of tobacco smoke that drifts between them, eyes scanning the room to ensure Joseph Vance remains preoccupied with the fallen stack of papers. 

"Smart man," Matthew Ross says, his voice losing some of its growl but none of its hardness. He finally pulls his hand away, stepping back just enough to give Robert space, though he remains positioned between Robert and the exit. "We've both seen enough blood for one lifetime, Robert. Don't go looking for ghosts in a graveyard that's already been paved over."


## Turn 6 — I pull out my leather-bound notebook and begin cross-referencing the contents of Case File #402 against the other visible folders on the shelves.

Robert Sullivan pulls the *Leather Bound Notebook* from his coat and flips through its pages, eyes darting between the handwritten notes and the visible spines of folders on the iron shelves. He begins comparing the dates and case numbers visible on the adjacent files to those recorded in the suspicious folder, searching for a pattern of substitution. 

Matthew Ross stands motionless, his heavy-lidded gaze tracking every movement of Robert's pen, creating a suffocating silence that even the teletype machine cannot break. 

Joseph Vance, still kneeling amidst the scattered papers on the floor, freezes mid-motion and looks up. His eyes lock onto Robert’s for a split second before he begins gesturing frantically toward a heavy, unmarked drawer at the bottom of a cabinet across the room, his hands trembling so violently that they knock a stray paper aside. 

The sudden, panicked movement draws Matthew Ross's attention away from Robert; the detective turns his head sharply toward the clerk, his brow furrowing in suspicion. Taking advantage of the distraction, Robert pushes past the heavy iron cabinets and lunges toward the unmarked drawer, his boots thudding against the floorboards as he scrambles across the cramped room. 

He reaches the cabinet and grips the cold metal handle of the unmarked drawer, wrenching it open with a harsh scrape that echoes through the precinct. 

The air in the new corner of the records room is stagnant and heavy with the scent of damp paper and old ink. One single, unlabelled manila folder sits alone at the very back of the drawer, its edges slightly charred.


## Turn 7 — I pull the charred manila folder from the drawer and quickly cross-reference its contents with the notes in my leather-bound notebook.

Robert Sullivan grabs the charred manila folder and flips it open, eyes darting between the singed pages and the scribbled entries in his *Leather Bound Notebook*. He searches for a specific name or date that matches the anomalies he found in Case File #402, but before his eyes can even settle on a line of text, heavy footsteps thunder toward him.

Matthew Ross lunges from his position by the filing cabinets, his face contorted into a mask of weary fury as he realizes Robert has bypassed him for the unmarked drawer. Joseph Vance scrambles backward on all fours, retreating into the shadows of the heavy iron shelves and leaving the center of the room wide open. 

The detective’s hand slams against the metal cabinet just inches from Robert's fingers, the impact ringing through the cramped room like a gunshot. 

"Drop it, Robert!" Matthew Ross bellows, his voice cutting through the rhythmic clatter of the teletype. 

Robert turns and bolts toward the heavy oak door of the records room, shoving past a startled Joseph Vance and stumbling into the main precinct hallway. He doesn't look back, his boots skidding on the polished linoleum as he pushes through the swinging double doors and into the main bullpen, where three uniformed officers stand mid-conversation under the harsh glare of the overhead electric lights.


## Turn 8 — I duck behind a heavy desk in the bullpen to avoid being spotted by the uniformed officers.

Robert Sullivan dives behind a heavy oak desk, pressing his back against the cold wood and pulling the charred manila folder tight against his chest. Three uniformed officers continue their conversation just feet away, their voices a dull drone of precinct gossip and complaints about the heat under the electric lights. One officer, a broad-shouldered man with a thick neck, laughs at something said by a second officer, a wiry man currently leaning over a stack of reports. A third officer, a woman with sharp eyes and a tightly coiled bun, turns her head toward the records room door for a split second before refocusing on her desk. 

The heavy swinging doors of the records room shudder as Matthew Ross bursts through them, his eyes scanning the expanse of the bullpen with frantic intensity. He stands in the center of the aisle, chest heaving, looking for any sign of movement that would betray Robert's position.


## Turn 9 — I stay pressed against the desk and peer around the edge to see if Matthew Ross is heading toward my position.

Robert Sullivan remains motionless behind the heavy oak desk, pressing his spine into the wood and clutching the charred manila folder against his ribs. He leans just far enough to peer around the corner of the desk, eyes darting through the gap toward the center of the aisle. 

Matthew Ross stands frozen in the open space, his gaze sweeping over the rows of desks with a heavy, searching stare. His shoulders rise and fall with jagged breaths, and the scent of stale tobacco follows him as he turns his head toward the cluster of three uniformed officers. The broad-shouldered officer with the thick neck stops laughing mid-sentence, his eyes narrowing as he looks toward Ross, while the wiry man pulls his hands away from the reports to straighten his posture. 

The woman with the sharp eyes and coiled bun stands up from her chair, her gaze shifting from her desk toward Matthew Ross, creating a heavy silence that cuts through the previous drone of gossip.


## Turn 10 — I slip behind the cluster of desks, keeping low to avoid being spotted by the officers, and attempt to get closer to the reports the wiry man was just touching.

Robert Sullivan crawls on hands and knees, pressing his chest toward the floorboards as he weaves through the maze of heavy oak legs and metal filing cabinets. His fingers brush against the edge of a desk just as the wiry officer pulls his hands away from the reports, eyes tracking the slight disturbance in the air near the cluster of workstations. 

The woman with the sharp eyes and coiled bun pushes her chair back, the wood screeching against the floor, and begins walking toward Robert's position with a measured, suspicious stride. Her gaze remains fixed on the gap between the desks where Robert is currently hunkered down, her boots clicking rhythmically against the linoleum. 

Matthew Ross turns his head sharply toward the sound of her approaching footsteps, his eyes widening as he realizes she is moving directly toward the area where Robert is hiding. He takes a heavy step forward, cutting off the path between Robert and the main exit, his shadow stretching long across the floor toward the crawling man.
