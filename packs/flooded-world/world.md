# World Bible — The Drowned Earth

## The Flood

The sea level rose two hundred feet over forty years. The cause is contested: glacial collapse, permafrost methane release, a combination of both. The result is the same. The coastal cities are gone. The inland valleys are underwater. The map has been redrawn by water.

It has been sixty years since the first evacuation orders. The world is divided into what is dry and what is not. The high ground holds the communities: mountain towns, plateau cities, ridge settlements. The low ground holds the drowned ruins: skyscrapers as reefs, highways as submerged lanes, suburbs as kelp forests.

The year is 2147. The flood is not receding. The climate is warmer, wetter, less predictable. The storms are bigger. The rain lasts longer. The water is the constant.

## The Geography

**The Highlands** are the habitable zone. Mountain towns, plateau cities, ridge settlements. The population lives here — maybe two hundred million people on the high ground. The terrain is rough, the arable land is scarce, and the communities are isolated.

**The Edge** is the transition zone: the hills that slope down into the flood, the last dry neighborhoods, the elevated highways that lead nowhere. The Edge is where the flood meets the land. It is fertile, contested, and dangerous.

**The Flooded Zones** are the drowned world. Cities underwater. Highways submerged. Suburbs become kelp forests. Skyscrapers are reefs — their lower floors are bioluminescent, their upper floors are bird nests, their rooftops are the only visible structure. The water is warm, biologically rich, and full of salvage.

**The Open Ocean** is the new normal. The old coastlines are gone. The ocean is warmer, more acidic, more alive. The storms are bigger. The currents are different. The fisheries are abundant but unpredictable.

## The Communities

**The Highland Councils** are the closest thing to government. They are coalitions of mountain towns and plateau cities. They have no standing army, only a militia. They have no unified currency, only trade agreements. They hold together by necessity and the understanding that isolation means starvation.

**The Floaters** are the boat communities. Houseboats, pontoon settlements, converted barges. They live on the residual lakes and the high rivers. They fish, they salvage, they trade. They answer to no council. They navigate by the water level and the storm forecasts.

**The Divers** are the salvage communities. They descend into the flooded zones for usable materials: copper wire, glass panels, structural steel, sealed containers. They are brave, they are well-paid, and they do not always come back. The drowned cities hold structural hazards, toxic sediment, and things that grew in the dark.

**The Raiders** are the people who take instead of trade. They are not numerous but they are effective. They hit the supply barges, they raid the Edge settlements, they terrorize the lowland routes. They are motivated by scarcity and the understanding that the world ended and the rules changed.

## The Economy

The economy runs on barter, labor, and localized currency. Fresh water is filtered but abundant in the highlands. Fish is abundant in the flood zones. Building materials are scarce — the old cities are underwater, and the highlands have no quarries.

Food is the primary constraint. The highlands have terraced farms and greenhouse domes. The Edge has flood-adapted crops. The Floaters have fish. But storage is the problem — without refrigeration, food spoils. Salt, smoke, and drying are the preservation methods.

Trade routes are the lifelines. Barges move goods on the rivers. Pack animals move goods on the high trails. Foot caravans move goods on the ridge paths. Each route has risks: storms, raiders, structural collapse, flooding.

## The Technology

**Boats** are the primary transport. Barges, houseboats, motorized skiffs, sail-canoes. The rivers are the highways. The flood zones are the open ocean. A working engine is valuable; a sail is reliable.

**Salvage tech** is the divers' toolkit: wetsuits, hand-lifts, cutting torches, sonar units, compressed-air tanks. The flooded zones are hazardous: structural collapse, toxic sediment, entanglement in submerged infrastructure.

**Agriculture** is the highlands' lifeblood: terraced farming, greenhouse domes, flood-adapted crop strains. The Edge has the most productive farmland but also the highest raid risk.

**Communication** is limited: radio on the rivers, signal flags on the trails, messenger birds in the highlands. The old internet is gone. The old telephone network is fragmented. The new network is slow and local.

## What Still Works

The highland terraces. The river barges. The salvage divers' equipment. The community granaries. The water filtration systems. The storm forecasts. The human instinct to adapt to the water, not against it.

## The Spatial Reality

The world is defined by elevation. The high ground is where you live. The Edge is where you work and risk everything. The flood is where you salvage and pray. The verticality is the defining feature: the highland towns are on ridges, the Edge settlements are on hills, the flooded cities are below. Every structure has a water line. Every community has an elevation.

The drowned cities are the most striking spatial feature: skyscrapers rising from the water, their lower floors bioluminescent and alive, their upper floors bird nests and weathered concrete. The submerged highways are underwater lanes marked by the tips of streetlights. The kelp forests grow in what were once parking garages. The water is warm and full of life.

Every community is isolated. Every route has risks. The flood is always there, rising and falling with the seasons, dictating where you can go and when.
