# World Bible — Cordyceps: Year Twenty

**Ophiocordyceps novum** mutated to human hosts twenty years ago. It did not announce itself. It spread through a batch of contaminated grain shipments, then through the airborne spores the fruiting bodies release in enclosed spaces. By the time anyone understood what they were looking at, the major cities were already gone. Not bombed, not burned — just lost to the fungus and the infected and the collapse of the systems that held everything together.

Twenty years is long enough for the world to look different. The cities are not ruins the way people once imagined ruins. They are forests. Trees have broken through parking garage roofs. Rivers have re-routed through subway systems. Apartment facades are draped in vines so thick they muffle gunshots. Deer and coyote and black bear move through streets that still have faded lane markings. The fungus is everywhere — blooming from bodies that were never cleared, threading through soil and root systems, pulsing in slow mycorrhizal networks that nobody fully understands yet but that every survivor knows to fear.

**The infected** are not mindless. The fungus is an organism with its own logic.

- **Runners** — early-stage, days to weeks post-infection. Still partly human. Their eyes are clouded but sometimes focused. They can still open doors. Sometimes they call out in voices that sound almost like the person they were. This is the worst thing about them.
- **Stalkers** — weeks to months. The fungus is branching into their musculature. They hide. They watch from shadows. They track a moving survivor for hours before they commit. They choose their moment.
- **Clickers** — months in, skull bloomed with fungal growth, eyes gone. Navigation by echolocation. They cannot see; they cannot be seen approaching in the dark. A single misplaced footstep will end the conversation.
- **Bloaters** — years of buildup. Slow, nearly unkillable without fire or concentrated impact, and they throw spore-pods. An enclosed space with a Bloater is a room you do not survive unless you run.

The mycelial network beneath the soil is the part that keeps experienced survivors awake at night. Disturbing a nest triggers a slow signal through the underground threads — hours later, dormant clusters kilometers away begin to stir. Nobody is sure how large the network is. The working theory is that it does not have an edge anyone has found yet.

**What is left of humanity** is scattered and brutal and occasionally extraordinary. There are settlements — some run by councils, some by warlords, some by religions that formed in the first five years and have calcified around their founders' particular terrors. The FEDRA military governs four major zones with martial law, checkpoints, and ration cards; inside the walls, medicine and food exist; outside them, you are on your own. The Fireflies were a resistance movement that believed a cure was possible — some still do, in cells scattered across the country. Most people are just people: trying to feed their families, making arrangements they are not proud of, keeping a small piece of the world from falling apart.

There is no internet, no phone grid, no central news. Information travels with people. Rumors are the primary currency. A shortwave radio is a treasure; a working vehicle is a small kingdom.

The children born in the last twenty years have never known the before-world. They are not haunted by it. They are practical, unsentimental, occasionally savage, occasionally capable of a kindness that breaks older survivors down entirely.
