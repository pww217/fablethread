The war is fought with rifles and railroads and the will to keep going when the rifle runs dry.
Language is formal but strained. Soldiers speak in understatement. Officers speak in orders and principles. Civilians speak in practical terms — food, shelter, survival.
The terrain matters. Rivers determine strategy. Railroads determine logistics. Hills determine battle lines.
Medicine is pre-scientific. Wounds infect. Amputation is common. Morphine is the painkiller. Quinine treats fever. Alcohol is the antiseptic.
The North has the factories and the railroads. The South has the terrain and the will to defend home ground. The border states are split down the middle.
Every town has a story. Every soldier has a reason for enlisting. The war is fought with rifles and railroads and the will to keep going.
Violence is sudden and devastating. Minie balls tear through flesh. Artillery shatters formations. The aftermath is worse than the battle.
Supply determines everything. An army marches on its stomach. Ammunition is counted and logged. Horses die of exhaustion. Wagons break down.
The telegraph is the most powerful weapon. Messages travel faster than armies. Information is strategic. A intercepted message can move a corps.
Enslaved people are navigating three competing authorities — Union, Confederate, and their own survival. Their choices shape the war.
The seasons matter. Spring floods close roads. Summer heat kills more than bullets. Winter freezes rivers and rail lines.
No one fights for abstract principles alone. They fight for home, for pay, for the person next to them, for the land they will lose.
