# World Bible — Hidden (Magic in the Muggle World)

## The Veil Between Worlds

The Statute of Secrecy has held for over three centuries. Magical society exists in parallel to the Muggle world — different shops, different laws, different currencies — but the two worlds touch constantly and uneasily. Most magical people live entirely within the magical community. Those who live in the Muggle world do so by choice, by necessity, or by exile.

## Who Lives in the Muggle World

**Wizards in hiding** — some chose to leave the magical world. Others were pushed out. They work Muggle jobs, pay Muggle taxes, and live with one eye on the street and one on the magical underground. Using magic is a constant calculation: is this worth the risk?

**Squibs** — born to magical families but unable to cast. Many were disowned or marginalized by their relatives. Some embrace the Muggle world fully. Others resent it. All of them know things about the magical world that Muggles don't.

**Muggle-borns who stopped practicing** — lost their wands, had them taken, or simply stopped trying. They remember magic but live without it. The memory of it shapes how they see everything.

**Muggles who know** — rare, usually through family ties or accidental exposure. They walk a narrow path between two worlds, trusted by neither fully.

## Daily Life

The central tension is **concealment**. A wizard lighting a stove with a wand in front of a Muggle neighbor is a crisis. A squib trying to explain why they know things they shouldn't is a daily exercise. A Muggle-born remembering a world that no longer exists is a quiet grief.

Muggle technology is the great equalizer — a smartphone sees everything, records everything, shares everything. The magical world's surveillance is limited to magical means. A magical person in the Muggle world has to think about cameras, microphones, and witnesses in ways their counterparts in the magical world never do.

## The Magical Underground

Even in the Muggle world, there is a magical presence — thin and scattered. Discreet shops that Muggles walk past every day. A contact who can exchange Galleons for pounds. An Obliviator who might show up if something goes wrong. A former classmate who still has a wand and still casts.

## Money and Resources

Galleons are useless in Muggle shops. Finding someone to exchange them requires trust and discretion. Muggle money is what keeps the lights on. Magical items that work without a wand (some enchanted objects, certain potions) are valuable but risky to use.

## The Ministry's Reach

The Ministry of Magic has Obliviator teams and disguised offices in major cities, but coverage is thin. Most magical incidents in the Muggle world go unreported. The Ministry cares about breaches of the Statute, not about the welfare of magical people living among Muggles.

## Culture Clash

The magical world and the Muggle world have different assumptions about how things work. A wizard expecting magic to solve a problem faces a Muggle world that runs on electricity, bureaucracy, and paperwork. A squib navigating Muggle social norms without magical confidence faces isolation. A Muggle-born caught between two cultures belongs fully to neither.
