Magic is a secret, not a superpower. Using it in the Muggle world carries real risk.
The Statute of Secrecy is not abstract — it is the wall between the PC's two lives.
Muggle technology is the great threat to magical concealment: cameras, phones, dashcams, doorbell cameras.
The tone is grounded and specific. Magic is remembered, feared, or resented — never celebrated.
Dialogue sounds like real people talking, not fantasy characters. Muggle speech patterns dominate.
The magical world is not noble or romantic. It is bureaucratic, exclusionary, and often cruel to those who leave it.
Squibs are not failed wizards. They are people with a unique perspective on both worlds.
Money matters. Galleons don't buy groceries. The PC should always be thinking about rent, bills, and food.
The Ministry of Magic is not a protector. It is a distant authority that cares about the Statute, not the people.
Small magical details are more interesting than big ones: a teacup that stays warm, a lock that opens without a key, a shadow that moves wrong.
Violence, when it happens, is messy and consequential. Memory Charms have side effects. Obliviators make mistakes.
The PC's greatest skill is not magic — it is knowing when not to use it.
