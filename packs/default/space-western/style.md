The Rim is dust, weld marks, and old grievances. Narrate the grit — boot prints on a wood-plank porch, the haze of a flickering oil lamp beside a holographic readout, the smell of axle grease and ozone in the same breath.

The Coalition is a presence felt more than seen — a flag on a courthouse, a fuel certificate signed by a clerk on another world, a cruiser overhead that never lands. When Coalition uniforms do appear in person, the temperature of a room changes.

Tech is mismatched and second-hand. Plasma carbines on the wall over a hand-cranked radio. A neural-link patch on a rancher whose son died at Saint-Albans. Show the seams. Things break and get welded together; nothing is shiny.

Speech is slow, regional, period-flavored without parody. People speak in idioms — "two moons short of broke," "owes the company a year and a sack of oats," "rode the long jump twice before breakfast." Mandarin, Spanish, and Plains-American mix freely. Avoid cosmic poetry; pilots speak in fuel weights and jump windows, deputies speak in warrant numbers and times of day.

Violence is close and consequential. A drawn pistol in a saloon means somebody dies; a fistfight on a freighter dock means somebody loses a hand to machinery. No clean shots. No bloodless brawls.

Travel is slow. Pulse-fold jumps take days. Sub-orbital hops take hours. The Rim is wide; nobody is in a hurry except the Coalition.

The war is still a fresh wound. Browncoats and loyalists drink at the same bars and pretend not to know each other. Some of them mean it. Veterans don't talk about Saint-Albans unless someone forces the conversation, and then they talk about it for a long time.

Faith is mixed and personal — a Shepherd reading scripture on a freighter, a Buddhist shrine and a Methodist chapel sharing a wall in a frontier town, a Companion's house two streets down. Don't lean on any single tradition or treat any of them as decorative.

Hope is small, local, and worth defending — a school staying open, a homestead surviving the dry season, a debt forgiven, a neighbor's secret kept. The Rim runs on these.

Frontier sheriffs, herd doctors, freight pilots, saloon keepers, terraforming engineers, Coalition tax assessors, junkers, Companion-guild registrants, and Browncoat veterans-turned-something-else are all part of the texture. Use them; don't overuse any one. Avoid the iconic captain-with-a-ship as the default — the Rim is bigger than its smugglers.
