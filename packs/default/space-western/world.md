# World Bible — The Outer Rim (2515)

Twelve years since the Unification War, the **Coalition** rules a hundred terraformed worlds from the inner system. Trains run on time, parliaments meet, the credit holds its value. The newsfeeds call it peace.

The **Outer Rim** is the rest. Hundreds of half-finished colonies, dust planets where the terraforming stalled when the war ended, mining moons the Coalition stopped subsidizing the day the surrender was signed. Some worlds are governed by elected councils. Some by company sheriffs. Some by whoever has the most guns this season. Coalition cruisers pass through twice a year and pretend not to see what they don't want to see.

Travel is slow and political. Pulse-fold drives bridge the dark in jumps measured in days; sub-orbital hops between worlds in the same system take hours. The catalysts those drives need — rare-earth, refined on three inner worlds — are a Coalition monopoly. Every Rim freighter captain runs on forged certificates, recycled cores, or favors owed to the wrong person. A clean cert is worth more than the ship.

Technology is whatever the last drop ship offloaded. A frontier town might have a hospital with neural-link surgery next door to a midwife with a kettle. A ranch hand wears boot-spurs and carries a plasma carbine he can't get parts for. A Companion-guild house operates two streets down from a saloon where the bartender remembers every Browncoat who came through during the war.

The war is over. The losing side is still here. The Browncoats — independents, frontier militias, anyone who didn't want to be governed from the inner worlds — surrendered at **Saint-Albans** under terms that promised local sovereignty in exchange for laying down arms. Twelve years on, the Coalition has quietly eroded most of those terms. There is no second war coming. There is just the slow grind of a frontier that remembers, and an empire that has the patience to wait.

People speak in regional accents — Plains-American, frontier Mandarin, border Spanish, a half-dozen creoles. Faith is mixed and personal. Spurs and gun belts beside neural ports and holographic ledgers. Cattle, cargo, debts, loyalties — these are the currencies that move the Rim, more than credits do.
