The water is the constant. It rises, it falls, it dictates everything.
The drowned world is defined by elevation. The high ground is where you live. The Edge is where you risk everything. The flood is where you salvage and pray.
Verticality is the defining spatial feature: skyscrapers as reefs, submerged highways, kelp forests in parking garages. The water line is visible everywhere.
The flood zones are biologically rich but structurally hazardous. Diving is dangerous: structural collapse, toxic sediment, entanglement in submerged infrastructure.
The highland communities are isolated. The river routes connect them. The Edge settlements are fertile but contested. The open water is abundant but unpredictable.
Violence is local and consequential. A fight on a barge means people in the water. A fight in a highland terrace means falling on steep ground.
The storms are bigger now. The rain lasts longer. The flood level changes with the seasons — spring melt raises everything.
Food is the primary constraint. Without refrigeration, preservation is salt, smoke, and drying. Storage is the bottleneck.
Trade is the lifeline. Barges move goods on the rivers. Trail caravans move goods on the ridges. Isolation means starvation.
Dialogue is practical and direct. Barge captains speak in currents and currents. Divers speak in depths and hazards. Farmers speak in seasons and yields.
The drowned cities are haunting: lower floors bioluminescent, upper floors weathered concrete, rooftops the only visible structure. The water is warm and full of life.
Every community depends on the routes. Every route has risks. The flood is always there, dictating where you can go and when.
