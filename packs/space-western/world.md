# World Bible — The Rim (Year 2187)

## The Setting

The inner system is corporate territory: Mars is a factory, Ceres is a data-hub, Luna is a military post. The Rim is what comes after — the outer Belt, the Jovian moons, the Kuiper fringe. Here the corps' reach ends and the frontier begins.

The Rim is built on remnants: generation ship habitats drifting through the Jovian system, asteroid stations built inside hollowed-out rock, orbital rings around gas giants that nobody maintains anymore. The technology works until it doesn't. The ships are patched together from salvage. The people are the same.

The year is 2187. The corps still control the inner system, but they can't project power this far. The Rim is autonomous by necessity, not ideology. Nobody declared independence. The corps just ran out of fuel for their patrols.

## The Generation Ships

The Argo-class generation ships were built to carry colonists to the outer system. They never arrived. Three are still drifting: the *Argos*, the *Hecate*, and the *Persephone*. Their populations live in the habitat rings, farming hydroponics, maintaining fusion reactors that are decades past their design life, and telling stories about the system they were supposed to reach.

The *Argos* is the largest — forty thousand souls in a spinning ring around a dead fusion core. It has a council, a militia, a dockyard. It has grain stores and hydroponic bays and a school. It also has food shortages and a reactor that is slowly losing containment.

The *Hecate* broke apart decades ago. Its remaining ring is held by independent operators, scavengers, and refugees. The interior is flooded sections and pressurized corridors. The exterior is a boneyard of derelict ships and salvage rigs.

The *Persephone* went dark two centuries ago. Nobody knows what happened. It drifts in the Jovian shadow, dark and silent. Some say it's still inhabited.

## The Orbital Rings

The gas giants have rings of their own — orbital stations built around Saturn, Jupiter, and beyond. These are the trade hubs: places where freight changes hands, where the corps' supply chains meet the Rim's black market, where politics is conducted in the space between a handshake and a gun.

The stations are built on engineering that was never meant to be permanent. The structures are aging. The structural members are fatigued. Nobody has the resources to reinforce them.

## The Faction Landscape

**The Rim Alliance** is the closest thing to government. It's a coalition of station councils, habitat leaders, and freight operators. It has no army, only a militia. It has no currency, only trade agreements. It holds together by consensus and the shared understanding that the corps are always looking for an opening.

**The Corps** still exist beyond the Rim. Protogen has mining operations on the outer moons. The MCRN has listening posts. They don't govern — they extract. They send haulers, not soldiers. But they always have leverage: medical supplies, reactor parts, data.

**The Independents** are everyone else: smugglers, scavengers, freelance pilots, station mechanics, data-brokers. They answer to no council and no contract. They survive on the margins: salvage, courier work, contraband, information.

**The Drifters** are the furthest out. Kuiper fringe dwellers who live on patched-together ships and ice-harvesting rigs. They don't trade. They don't trust. They survive by being invisible.

## The Economics

The Rim economy runs on barter and credit-chits. The corps mint their own currency; the Rim uses a mix. Water, fuel, and reactor parts are the real currencies. A working fusion core is worth more than a station full of food. A data-packet with verified information is worth more than a cargo of weapons.

Dock fees exist but are negotiated. Corporate haulers pay the Alliance toll; independent operators slip through. The militia collects protection from the bigger stations. The smaller ones pay in favors, not credits.

## The Technology

**Ships** are the lifeblood. A working ship is worth more than a station. Ships are patched, modified, and maintained by crews who understand more than they should. The Epstein Drive is standard but rare this far out. Most ships run on modified fusion drives or solar-sail arrays.

**Weapons** range from kinetic pistols and shotgun-rifles to rail-gun variants and point-defense lasers. Ammunition is specific. Energy cells are recharged but finite. Corporate weapons are regulated; rim-built weapons are unregistered and unreliable.

**Cyberware** exists but is rare. The medical infrastructure to maintain augmentations is thin. Black-market implants work but carry rejection risk. The generation ships have clinics; the fringe does not.

## What Still Works

The fusion reactors — barely. The hydroponics. The water recyclers. The comms array. The hull plating. The human instinct to patch, modify, and keep things running one more cycle.

## The Spatial Reality

The Rim is defined by space — pressurized corridors, vacuum on the other side of a thin wall, the spin-gravity of a ring that keeps turning because nobody stopped it. The stations are labyrinths: habitat rings, fuel depots, dry docks, flooded sections, collapsed corridors. The generation ships are vertical cities with spin gravity and hydroponic bays. The freighters are small and patched and the only thing between you and the void.

Every structure is aging. Every hull is degrading. Every airlock is one bad seal from disaster. The frontier is not empty; it is full of things that are falling apart and the people who refuse to let them.
