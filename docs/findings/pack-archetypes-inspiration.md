## 1. Noir — 1930s

**File:** `packs/default/noir-1930s/`
**Description:** "Detective in a Depression-era city. Every case is different."
**Tone tags:** noir, urban, procedural, moral-ambiguity
**Names:** Male only
**Currency:** 50 dollars
**Inventory:** 4–8 items, must include weapon + tool
**Prose:** 530–930 words

### World Bible (world.md)
- Great Depression: 20–25% unemployment, New Deal uncertain, cash/favors as currency
- Prohibition ended 1933 but bootlegging infrastructure remains — organized crime diversified into labor racketeering, gambling, loan-sharking, protection
- City = overlapping jurisdictions (police, sheriff, state troopers, federal, private) — each different scope/rules, people on payroll
- Police = mix of idealists and careerists, significant portion taking payback

### Baseline Facts (3)
1. City runs on violence and money — gangsters control docks/unions/night. Police take cut. Ask too many questions = bullet or frame-up.
2. Every man has an angle — loyalty bought and sold, betrayal for money.
3. City at night is a character — rain, shadows, alleys, distant train, unseen danger.

### World Facts (3)
1. City gripped by corruption spanning police, politics, organized crime — no reform can touch it
2. Prohibition created black market funding speakeasies, bootlegging, smuggling
3. Depression drove desperate to crime; wealthy insulate behind influence

- Pirate communities (Nassau, Port Royal) = self-governing havens with rules, courts, distribution

### Baseline Facts (3)

3. Caribbean = maze of hidden coves, shallow reefs, coral harbors. Best pirates ambush, strike fast, vanish into navy-unfollowable waters.

### World Facts (3)
1. Seas controlled by powerful pirate fleets and colonial navies clashing over trade routes/island holdings

## Key Observations

### What's structurally identical across all packs:
- All have exactly 14 arc_categories, 8 character_dynamics, 8 npc_bonds, 7 scene_detail_bundles, 4 pc_situation_schema fields
- All use `min_objectives_per_quest: 2`, `starting_quest_count: 1`
- All prose range is 530–930 words
- All scene bundles follow same 2-field structure (conditions, sensory)
- All NPC bonds follow same pattern: id, tags, description
> **Note:** `moral_pressures` was removed in July 2025 — it was passed to the seed LLM with no structured place to use it (the `thematic_question` field it was supposed to feed into was removed). The pool was dead weight.

### What's different:
- **Baseline facts** — the only real source of flavor. Noir has the best 3-line vignettes. WW2 is also strong. Zombie has the most detailed baseline facts (specific infection mechanics). Space Western is decent but generic "frontier" language. Piracy is strong on the democracy/violence tension.
- **Situation archetypes** — Noir's are the most specific and evocative ("murder_investigation", "heist_aftermath", "judicial_rigging"). WW2's are also strong ("retreat_through_burning_village", "paratrooper_drop_zone"). Zombie's are the weakest — abstract bureaucratic labels ("infrastructure_failure", "resource_rationing", "social_betrayal").
- **Arc categories** — All are weak here. Almost all are abstract noun phrases with zero evocative language. "institutional_corruption", "power_struggle", "resource_scarcity" — these read like database columns, not story seeds.
- **NPC bonds** — The descriptions are the strongest part across all packs. They read like actual prose. But the IDs are abstract.
- **Scene detail bundles** — Noir and WW2 have the best sensory details. Zombie's are decent. Space Western's sci-fi elements feel generic ("grav pallet", "hydro-spanner"). Piracy's are period-appropriate but sometimes feel like museum exhibits.
- **PC situation schema** — Very procedural. "Where do you live?" "What's your reputation?" "Who's in charge?" — reads like a character sheet, not a story prompt.
